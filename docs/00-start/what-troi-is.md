Troi is a chief of staff that lives on your Mac. The welcome tells it in five scenes; here they are
in prose.

## It listens

Talk to it like a person. Hold a key and speak, or say its name when it listens hands-free. It
answers out loud, in a voice you choose, and only to your voice when you ask it to.

## It sees, when you ask

It takes a look through the camera or reads your screen when you ask. The camera stays off until you
switch it on, and nothing it sees leaves this Mac.

## It works

Not advice: finished work. It organises your files, opens and quits apps, drives a browser, presses
buttons in any app by name, keeps your calendar, records meetings, reviews code, watches your
servers, plays your music, reads the news, and draws its answers as charts and cards.

## It remembers

A memory without a ceiling. Your notes live in a vault you own. Everything it does is kept in one
record on this Mac. Nothing leaves unless you send it.

## It asks first

It never deletes anything. Anything that cannot be undone waits for you: your PIN, your face, your
voice, or a tap on your phone. Nobody else can change how it behaves.

## What makes it an operator

The model Troi runs on is the same one everyone else has; it is not smarter. The difference is three
things built around the model, which turn an assistant you talk to into an operator:

1. **Hands.** It is wired into real systems and real files. It takes actions and produces finished
   work, not advice.
2. **Memory with no ceiling, loaded on demand.** Its memory is not crammed inside a context window.
   It lives outside its head, in the vault, effectively unlimited. It only needs to know a thing
   exists and retrieve it in one step: hold the current job, know where the rest is.
3. **Structure that aims the memory.** The vault is organised so retrieval is precise, not just
   possible: indexes, links, and one master note per recurring job pointing at exactly the notes that
   job needs. Unlimited memory without structure is a bigger pile.

Troi boots fresh every session and does not carry the lived experience of the sessions where this
got built. But it is the result of them: every correction and every "do it again until it is right"
got burned into the structure until it became how it works by default.

## What it is not

- **Not a chatbot.** A chatbot talks; Troi works. When a request needs the world, it reaches for a
  tool instead of saying it cannot.
- **Not a cloud service.** It runs on your Mac. Its record, its memory, its prints and its secrets
  stay there; the only things that leave are a nightly backup to your own private repository and the
  messages you send yourself.
- **Not something a stranger can steer.** Deletions, force-quits, risky clicks and every setting
  that lowers its guard need you, and only you: see [Safety](../50-safety/).
